What stays private

Prove the answer. Keep the records.

Three parties, three different views. Your device does the private work; the verifier checks the claim off-chain; the buyer receives only the answer you approved.

Stays on your device
  • Raw transaction CSV
  • Private witness values
  • The issuer's blinding value
  • Your exact spending total

Mock Grocer receives one thing from you: a commitment to a key your browser creates, so it can issue the signed credential.

Used by the verifier
  • Signed credential
  • Zero-knowledge proof
  • Public campaign parameters
  • Consent receipt
  • One-time claim tag (replay protection)
  • Your payout address

Current limit: the credential root is visible to the verifier, so two claims from one statement can be linked.

Why Zcash

Privacy should extend to the payment.

When someone is rewarded for proving a private fact, the payment itself can reveal information. VeilBounty explores shielded Zcash settlement so that paying for a verified claim does not unnecessarily disclose who qualified.

  1. 1 · Your device

    Private data processing

    Records are read and proved in your browser.

  2. 2 · Operator's verifier

    Off-chain claim verification

    Signature, proof, consent and payout binding are checked by the verifier — not by Zcash.

  3. 3 · Zcash testnet

    Shielded payment

    The reward is meant to settle to a shielded address.

    Simulated by default · no transaction sent

Zcash does not verify the proof and is not an escrow here: the operator holds campaign funds and decides when to pay. In manual mode the operator sends TAZ from their own wallet and reports the transaction id, which the app shows as reported, not confirmed.

Designed around consent

Your participation. Your decision.

Review the campaign's purpose, understand the requested disclosure, and approve the claim before submission.

Withdrawing stops future use under the campaign terms, but cannot recall an answer already delivered to a buyer. Retention, purpose and no-resale limits are contractual terms, not cryptographic guarantees.

Questions, answered plainly

Is this a live marketplace?

No. It is an interactive prototype built for Zecathon. The merchant, its records and the buyer are synthetic, and everything runs on Zcash testnet.

Does anyone receive my transactions?

Not the verifier and not the buyer. Your browser keeps the rows and the total. The verifier receives the claim package — proof, credential, consent receipt, public inputs and payout address — and the buyer receives the approved answer.

Am I anonymous?

No. The verifier can link claims made from the same statement through the visible credential root, and your network address is visible to the services you contact.

Will I actually get paid?

In this build, payouts are simulated and no Zcash transaction is sent. A manual mode lets the operator pay from their own testnet wallet and report the transaction; the app does not verify it on-chain.

Can someone cheat by editing records or reusing a claim?

The demo includes a security lab that tries exactly that — edited rows with the same total, replaying a claim, and redirecting the payout — and shows each one fail. Edited rows no longer match the merchant's signature, so no proof can be built; the replay and the redirect are rejected by the verifier.
